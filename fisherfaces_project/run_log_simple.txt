  PART 2: eigenfaces vs Fisherfaces on real faces
=====================================================

-----------------------------------------------------
  ORL
-----------------------------------------------------
  d = 10304 pixels, N = 192 training, 128 test, c = 32 people

  1. correlation (raw pixels)      : 91.4%
  2. eigenfaces, best k = 50       : 95.3%
  3. eigenfaces at k = c-1 = 31    : 94.5%
  4. FISHERFACES (31 features)     : 90.6%

  checks:
    (a) ||S_T - (S_B+S_W)|| / ||S_T|| = 5.517e-16   (should be ~1e-16)
    (b) rank(S_B) = 31, bound c-1 = 31, we kept 31
    (c) off-diagonal share of W'*W     (Euclidean) = 4.986e-01
        off-diagonal share of W'*S_W*W (S_W-inner)  = 3.787e-13
        => S_W-orthogonal, not Euclidean-orthogonal

    (c) off-diagonal share of W'*W     (Euclidean) = 4.123e-01

================ SUMMARY (recognition rate %) ================
  database    correlation   eig(best)   eig(c-1)   FISHERFACES
  ORL              91.4        95.3       94.5          90.6
  Yale             43.0        34.2       29.5          67.2
==============================================================

  Read it like this:
   - On Yale (illumination only) Fisherfaces win by a wide margin.
     That is the paper's claim, reproduced.
   - On ORL (pose and expression) Fisherfaces LOSE to eigenfaces.
     Reason: ORL gives 6 training images per person, so each class
     contributes only n_j - 1 = 5 degrees of freedom to S_W.  Yale
     gives 40, so 39.  S_W is far better estimated on Yale, and
     Fisherfaces depend on inverting it.
   - On Yale, plain correlation BEATS eigenfaces.  PCA actively
     hurts there, because its leading components encode lighting.
