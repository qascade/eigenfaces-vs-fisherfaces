=== E2: label permutation sanity check (ORL) ===
                       true labels   shuffled labels
  eigenfaces (k=c-1)       94.5%            0.8%
  Fisherfaces              90.6%            0.0%
  chance (100/c)            3.1%            3.1%

  Both collapse to chance or below, which is what we want to see:
  the recognition rates come from the faces, not from the plumbing.

  Why BELOW chance rather than exactly at it?  Because the shuffle
  is applied to training labels only.  Fisherfaces faithfully learns
  to separate the shuffled grouping, so a test face is mapped near
  the training images that look like it -- and those now carry
  somebody else's label by construction.  The method works; it has
  just been told the wrong thing, and it is systematically wrong.

   Error climbs with angle for both methods, and the gap between
   them WIDENS: that is the paper's central claim, that Fisherfaces
   hold up where illumination gets extreme.

   Subset 1 (0-12 deg) is EMPTY, and that is not a bug: it is the
   split bias showing up again.  dir sorts names as text and '+'
   sorts before '-', so the first 40 files per subject -- the
   training set -- swallow all the near-frontal lighting.  Every
   test image is therefore at least 12 degrees off axis, which is
   precisely why the headline Yale numbers look so poor.
